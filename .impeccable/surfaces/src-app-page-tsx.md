# Landing page

**Scope:** the public route `/`. Visitor mode: **Persuade**.

**Audience and job.** A freelancer or independent contractor holding a client
agreement they cannot negotiate, usually days from a signing deadline, who has
been burned by a contract before. They want to know what they are accepting and
what to say back.

**The one action.** Put a document in, in its working form: a paste-or-upload
entry on the first viewport, not a link pointing at one further down.

**Proof and content.** The worked example is the synthetic agreement in
`gold-set/sample-contractor-agreement/`, with its seven recorded flags, their
tiers, consequences, counter-offers, and verbatim source sentences. It is
labelled synthetic wherever a visitor could mistake it for a real client's
contract. No prices, customers, testimonials, benchmarks, or logos exist and
none may be invented.

**Constraints.** No verdict on whether to sign. No legal advice and no
enforceability opinion. No scanned or photographed documents. Only the document
types the brief serves: contract, lease, freelance agreement, terms of service.
Vocabulary is governed by `CONTEXT.md`; the words review, scan, risk score,
enforceable, unenforceable, legal advice, and chat never appear. Severity is
three words, never a number. All reader-facing copy passes the humanizer skill
before it is committed.

**Memorable moment.** Selecting a line locks its quoted sentence into alignment
beside it. Alignment is the found-it state.

**Unresolved.** Pricing, the OpenRouter model, and whether the question box may
answer from the summary are open product decisions and are not claimed here.

## Direction contract

**THESIS.** The contract read back as a statement of account: every clause a
line item, ranked by what it costs the reader, each entry carrying the
document's own sentence as its reference. It refuses the category arrangement —
headline and subcopy beside a shadowed document screenshot, three feature cards
under it — and refuses the hero-metric template that would turn severity into a
score.

**OWN-WORLD.** Ledger stock as the ground, in the pale columnar green a real
accounting pad is printed on, never white and never cream; the statement sheet a
lighter panel ruled in ledger green. Ink near-black. Ledger green owns the
structure: column rules, heads, and the entry frame. Red is reserved the way a
statement reserves it, for what this costs you, and carries Critical alone.
Carbon violet marks copies and secondary notes. Tiers never rely on colour: each
carries its word and its own accounting rule — double rule for Critical, single
for Serious, dotted leader for Worth knowing. Condensed grotesk caps for column
heads and the masthead, tabular figures throughout, a Times-metric serif for
every specimen of the document itself. The scene is a kitchen table at eleven at
night with twenty minutes of dense text ahead, which is why the ground is a
tinted sheet rather than a lit white one.

**STORY.** The visitor understands that Redline turns a document they cannot
change into a ranked account of what it commits them to. They believe it because
every line quotes the document and they can find that sentence in their own
copy. They paste a document.

**FIRST VIEWPORT.** One statement, asymmetric, nothing centred. The masthead
runs the full width: the product name set as the account head, one line saying
what it does, and the entry field — paste or drop a document — occupying the
right of the head where a statement puts its account number. Immediately beneath
it the worked example opens as the statement body: column heads in ledger green,
then the first line item complete, its clause, its consequence, its quoted
sentence in the reference column, its tier in the amount column, and the
counter-offer that belongs to it, with the next line beginning beneath. The
reader sees the mechanism before the fold, not a promise of it.

Measured at 1440 wide, after the colour and spacing pass of 2026-10-02: the
first entry's counter-offer ends at 883px and the second line begins at 904px.
The product owner asked for more whitespace and larger section names, which
costs the counter-offer its place on a short first viewport; the clause with
its quoted sentence and its tier still clears comfortably, and that is the part
the promise is about. The trade was made deliberately and in that order:
breathing room first, the counter-offer's position second.

Amended 2026-10-02, after the first finish review scored this block partial. The
block asked for two line items. One complete entry carrying its counter-offer
demonstrates more of the product than two entries whose counter-offers are both
below the fold, and buying the second entry cost the masthead more space than
the second entry was worth. The amendment is a decision, not a concession to
what happened to be built: the fold target is a full first entry.

**FORM.** Statement of Account, candidate 1 of the ordered grounded list, taken
by the user from the pick card over the roll's assignment (The Galley Proof,
index 3). Seed key ed6ffbb5. Five disciplines carry over from the hands the
round beat: ink owning whole fields rather than accents; one truth per entry,
never a stacked badge; asymmetry as compositional law; tier never encoded by
colour alone; and two columns that lock into alignment as the found-it state.
