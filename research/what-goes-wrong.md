## 1. Auto-renewal / Negative-option / Cancellation Barriers

- FTC: "In 2024 the Commission received nearly 70 consumer complaints per day on average, up from 42 per day in 2021" regarding negative-option/subscription practices.
- FTC's final Click-to-Cancel rule "rests on copious evidence—including dozens of enforcement actions, tens of thousands of consumer complaints, comments of industry and consumer groups, and economic studies—showing that unfair and deceptive negative option practices are prevalent," with more than 100,000 complaints over the preceding five years.
- CFPB warned that negative-option programs may violate the Consumer Financial Protection Act when sellers "mislead consumers who want to cancel, place unreasonable barriers to cancellation, or fail to honor cancellation requests."

Sources:
https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/10/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-click-cancel-rule-making-it-easier-consumers-end-recurring
https://www.zwillgen.com/auto-renewal/cfpb-seeking-to-make-its-mark-on-subscription-and-auto-renewal-practices/

## 2. Late Fees / Penalty Fees

- CFPB: credit card issuers charged $12 billion in late fees in 2020, rising to $14.5 billion in 2022 (a 28% year-over-year increase).
- Companies charged as much as $41 per missed payment before the CFPB's rule; a 2023 Consumer Reports national survey found roughly 1 in 5 Americans (~52 million people) paid a credit card late fee in the past year.
- CFPB's final rule cut the typical late fee from $32 to $8, projected to save families more than $10 billion annually.

Sources:
https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-bans-excessive-credit-card-late-fees-lowers-typical-fee-from-32-to-8/
https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-initiates-review-of-credit-card-company-penalty-policies-costing-consumers-12-billion-each-year/

## 3. Non-payment / Freelancer Payment Terms

- Freelancers Union surveyed 5,358 freelancers (July 2015): half had trouble getting paid in the prior year; 71% had struggled to collect payment at least once in their career; 59% were owed $50,000 or more in unpaid invoices from late-paying clients.
- Only 28% of freelancers use a written contract for any given project, but those who do cut payment disputes by 73%.

Source:
https://www.onlabor.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/FU_NonpaymentReport_r3.pdf

## 4. Arbitration Clauses / Class-Action Waivers

- Arbitration clauses with class-action waivers appear across virtually all major consumer contract categories: credit cards, cell phone service, online retailers, social media platforms, rideshare apps, gym memberships, internet/software service agreements, loan agreements, and bank accounts.
- "Most consumer arbitration clauses include a provision stating you agree not to join class action lawsuits," and these are described as often the only practical mechanism to address small harms affecting many people — which the waiver forecloses.
- The Supreme Court has interpreted the Federal Arbitration Act as generally making these waivers enforceable even for claims too small to arbitrate individually.

Sources:
https://consumersadvisory.org/consumer-rights/what-to-know-about-arbitration-clauses-in-consumer-contracts
https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12764

## 5. Non-compete Clauses

- FTC estimated approximately 20% of U.S. workers are bound by a noncompete agreement; another FTC-sourced figure put it at 18% of the workforce, or about 30 million people.
- Senior executives (under 0.75% of workers) were carved out of the FTC's ban for existing agreements, but new noncompetes were banned outright for everyone under the rule as proposed.
- The FTC received more than 26,000 public comments on the proposed rule, with over 25,000 in support of banning noncompetes — indicating broad public grievance.
- FTC separately brought enforcement actions against three companies and two individuals for imposing noncompete restrictions on thousands of workers.

Sources:
https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/fact-sheet-ftcs-proposed-final-noncompete-rule
https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-cracks-down-companies-impose-harmful-noncompete-restrictions-thousands-workers

## 6. Personal Guarantees (Commercial Leases)

- Practitioner guidance describes personal guarantees as a routine feature of small-business commercial leases: "most commercial landlords require personal guarantees from small businesses because LLCs with no track record offer little real security."
- The risk cited: "if you signed a 10-year lease and personally guaranteed it, you could be on the hook for the full balance — even if your business closes after a year," and the obligation "is designed to survive the business's failure — even the entity's bankruptcy does not by itself erase the guarantor's personal liability."
- Negotiation guidance (limiting the guarantee period to 1-2 years rather than full lease term) is commonly recommended, implying this is a frequent point of friction.

Source:
https://leaselens.org/blog/personal-guarantee-explained

## 7. IP Assignment Clauses (Freelance Contracts)

- Freelance contract red-flag guides identify IP assignment as a standard clause category present in "most client contracts," which "transfers ownership to the client."
- The specific recurring risk flagged: "the red flag is when ownership transfers before full payment" is received, leaving freelancers exposed if a client doesn't pay but still owns the work product.

Source:
https://www.workcontractreview.com/blog/freelance-contract-red-flags

## 8. Kill Fees (Freelance/Creative Contracts)

- Identified in freelance-contract structuring guides as a named, recurring clause type: "a kill fee is a predetermined amount the client agrees to pay if they cancel the project after work has begun."
- Referenced alongside IP assignment and payment terms as one of the standard clauses freelance-contract-review resources instruct freelancers to check for, implying it is a frequent point of dispute or omission.

Source:
https://www.jobbers.io/the-most-common-freelance-contract-clauses-that-prevent-disputes-ranked-by-frequency/
